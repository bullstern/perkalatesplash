Your loyalty program only talks to people who already know you

I'm a fan of loyalty programs. The data says they're working better than they ever have, and if you have one that's running well, you should be proud of it.

But there's a limit built into every program, and I don't hear it talked about enough. A loyalty program can only reward someone who has already found you. It's a retention tool. It was never designed to bring in the shopper who hasn't bought yet, and no amount of tuning will change that.

Programs are performing at a high level

First, the good news, because it's real.

Antavo's Global Customer Loyalty Report 2026 found 83.0% of loyalty program owners are satisfied with their program's performance, 92.7% report a positive return and the average ROI is 5.3x. Nearly 9 in 10 brands (89.4%) say their program delivers value they wouldn't capture otherwise.

On the consumer side, the 2026 Bond Loyalty Report, based on 20,591 US loyalty members, found 85% of consumers are more likely to keep doing business with a brand that has a loyalty program, and 73% spend more as a result.

And the brand-level results from Smile.io's customer stories are striking. Applied Nutrition members are worth 215% more in lifetime value than non-members (Smile.io). Henry's House of Coffee members deliver 175% higher customer lifetime value (Smile.io).

So the tool works. The question is who it reaches.

The ceiling on every program

Look at those same case studies from a different angle.

Henry's House of Coffee's program covers 58% of its active customer base (Smile.io). That's a strong number. It also means the program's audience is, at best, the brand's existing customers.

Applied Nutrition has an engaged base of nearly 27,000 members out of a customer base of more than 120,000 (Smile.io). The program's job is to get more of those 120,000 to act like members. Valuable work, but every one of them has already bought.

Even the best-run program is capped by the size of your customer list. If you want to grow faster than your list, you need a way to reach people who aren't on it.

Why you can't fix this from inside the program

The usual moves to grow a program, like referral rewards, sign-up bonuses or richer tiers, are good ideas. Applied Nutrition launched referral rewards so loyal customers have a reason to bring in the next buyer (Smile.io). Referrals do reach new people.

But they reach new people through your existing customers' networks, on your existing customers' schedule. You don't get to choose who they refer, and you can't aim them at a specific kind of shopper.

There's also a gap between how brands and customers see these programs. Antavo found 82.6% of marketers believe loyalty programs make customers feel valued, but only 56.2% of customers agree (Antavo). If your own members aren't fully convinced, it's a lot to ask them to sell the program for you.

The shoppers you want are loyal to someone else

Here's the thing that makes this solvable. Your best prospects are already in loyalty programs. Just not yours.

Antavo found 65.9% of consumers say loyalty programs are now part of their everyday lives (Antavo). The shopper who'd love your LA skincare brand might well be an Ulta Platinum or Diamond member. The one who'd love your activewear might be a lululemon member at Collective Plus or Pinnacle. Their status in those programs, plus where they've spent in the last 90 days, says a lot about whether they'd like you.

How the two work together

I don't see this as a replacement for your program. I see it as the front door.

Funded rewards bring in the first purchase. Pick a segment, say Sephora Beauty Insiders at VIB or Rouge whose cards also show luxury retail spend in the last 90 days, and fund a reward worth having.

Your program keeps them. Once they've bought, they're on your list, and everything your program does well starts working on them too.

Your data improves both. As new customers come in, you learn which outside segments turned into your best members. Use that to tighten the next segment.

Questions worth asking your team

If you run a loyalty program, here are a few questions I'd bring to your next planning meeting:

1. What share of our revenue comes from people who were members before their most recent purchase? 2. Where did our best members come from originally? 3. If we wanted to double the number of new customers next year, which channel would we use, and what would it cost per customer? 4. Which other loyalty programs are our best customers likely in?

The last question is the one most teams haven't asked. It's also the one that points to the shoppers you don't have yet.

The takeaway

Keep investing in your loyalty program. It's doing its job. Just be clear about what that job is. It rewards the people who already know you. To reach the people who don't, you need something that starts outside your list.
