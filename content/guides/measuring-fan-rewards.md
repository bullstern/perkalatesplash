# Measuring Fan Rewards: Viewed, Claimed, Redeemed

How to measure sponsor funded fan rewards with a simple funnel, what each stage tells you, and what to expect in a first season.

Sponsors renew what they can measure. If a fan reward program can't produce clear numbers, it becomes a line item that's easy to cut. The good news is that the measurement doesn't need to be complicated. Three numbers carry most of the story.

### The three stage funnel

**Viewed.** How many eligible fans saw the reward. This is your reach among verified fans, a far more useful number than raw impressions because every view came from someone who qualifies.

**Claimed.** How many fans took the reward and saved it. A claim is an active choice. It tells you the offer was relevant enough to act on.

**Redeemed.** How many fans actually used it, in person or online. This is the number sponsors care about most, because it means a fan bought or did something.

Track all three for every reward, and look at the drop between each stage.

### Reading the drop offs

* **Low views:** a placement problem. The reward isn't where fans are looking. Move it to the gate, the concourse screens, the team app or the email after the game.
* **Views but few claims:** an offer problem. The reward isn't compelling or isn't clear. Test a different value, a clearer headline, or a better match between sponsor and moment.
* **Claims but few redemptions:** a friction or timing problem. The redemption window may be too short, the location inconvenient, or the steps too many. Make it easier to use on the spot or online.

Each drop off points to a specific fix, which is why the funnel is more useful than a single redemption count.

### Compare against the right baseline

The question a sponsor really asks is whether rewarding verified fans beats what they did before. Useful comparisons:

* Redemption rate on a verified fan reward against a public promo code the same sponsor ran last season.
* Redemptions on game days against redemptions at the venue on off days.
* Claims from season ticket members against claims from other registered fans.

You don't need perfect attribution. You need a fair comparison that a sponsor's marketing team will recognize.

### What to expect in a first season

Be honest with sponsors about the curve. Early rewards teach you more than they earn.

* **The first few weeks** are about learning which placements get views. Expect to move rewards around.
* **By midseason** you should know which sponsors and moments get claims and which don't. Cut the weak ones.
* **By the end of the season** you should have a short list of rewards that redeem well, with numbers to support them.

Set expectations around learning, not around a target redemption rate you can't yet justify. Sponsors respect a team that tests and reports clearly more than one that overpromises.

### Report without sharing identities

None of this requires personal data. Aggregate counts for viewed, claimed and redeemed are enough to value an activation, compare rewards and make renewal decisions. Keeping reporting at the audience level protects fans and keeps your team out of uncomfortable conversations about who received which list.

A simple end of season report for each sponsor can fit on one page: rewards run, eligible fans reached, claims, redemptions, the best performing moment, and what you'd change next season.

### Plan a pilot

Choose one audience, one reward and one result to measure. Set the budget and eligibility rules before launch. Tell participants which information you need and share results in aggregate where possible.
