# Access beats discounts when the perk matters

Test early access, priority booking, or a useful upgrade before cutting price.

A discount changes the price of a purchase. An access perk changes what a customer can do. Early product access, a reserved appointment slot or a small upgrade can feel valuable if it solves a real problem for the people receiving it.

### Choose access people will use

Ask what customers already wait for or try to reserve. A beauty shop could offer a preview appointment. A retailer could give members a head start on a limited release. Make the rules, availability and expiration clear before someone claims the perk.

### Test the same audience and timing

Compare the access perk with your usual reward on similar groups. Keep placement and timing consistent. Track how many people claim and use each offer, how much it costs to fulfill, and how many return.

### Protect the experience

Set a cap the team can handle, give staff one sentence that explains the offer, and provide a clear fallback if inventory runs out. A perk that repeatedly disappoints costs more than the discount it replaced.
