# Measuring Member Perks: What to Track and What to Expect

How gyms and studios can measure member perks with a viewed, claimed, redeemed funnel, plus realistic expectations for the first 90 days.

A perk program that can't show results gets cut the first time budgets tighten, and partners stop funding rewards they can't measure. The fix isn't a complex dashboard. It's a handful of numbers you check on a regular rhythm.

### Start with the funnel

Every perk moves through three stages.

**Viewed.** How many eligible members saw it. If this is low, the perk is in the wrong place.

**Claimed.** How many members took it. If views are healthy and claims are low, the offer isn't compelling or clear enough.

**Redeemed.** How many members used it. If claims are healthy and redemptions are low, something is in the way: the window is too short, the partner is inconvenient, or there are too many steps.

Watch the drop between each stage. Each one points to a different fix.

### Then connect it to attendance

Funnel numbers tell you whether the perk worked as an offer. Attendance tells you whether it worked for your business. Track these alongside:

* **Off peak fill rate:** attendance in the slots you targeted, compared with the same slots in the month before.
* **Second visit rate:** the share of new members who came back within 14 days, before and after you introduced perks.
* **Return after a gap:** the share of lapsed members who checked in again within a month of seeing a perk.

You don't need perfect attribution. You need a fair before and after comparison on the slots and groups you targeted.

### Report to partners at the audience level

Partners funding rewards want to know whether your members engaged. Give them aggregate counts for viewed, claimed and redeemed, plus the times and locations where redemptions happened. They don't need names, and your members will trust you more knowing names aren't shared.

A one page monthly summary works well: perks run, eligible members reached, claims, redemptions, best performing slot, and what you'll change next month.

### What to expect in the first 90 days

Be realistic. The first month is mostly learning.

* **Days 1 to 30:** You'll find out which placements get views. Test the front desk and instructor mentions against email, and expect to move things around.
* **Days 31 to 60:** You'll see which partners and offers get claims. Drop the weakest and double down on the best.
* **Days 61 to 90:** You should be able to compare off peak attendance and second visit rates against your starting baseline, with enough data to decide what to keep.

Don't set a target redemption rate before you have your own baseline. Your members, your schedule and your partners are different from anyone else's.

### Common mistakes

* **Measuring only redemptions.** You miss whether the problem was reach, offer or friction.
* **Changing everything at once.** Change one variable per slot so you know what moved the number.
* **Sending perks to everyone.** A perk for all members is a discount, and it muddies the comparison.
* **Waiting too long to review.** Check every two weeks so weak perks don't run for a whole quarter.

### Plan a pilot

Choose one audience, one reward and one result to measure. Set the budget and eligibility rules before launch. Tell participants which information you need and share results in aggregate where possible.
