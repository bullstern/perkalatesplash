# The Second Order Playbook for Online Brands

How online brands turn first time buyers into repeat customers: reorder timing, offer design, who gets an incentive, and where to put it.

For most online brands, the first order is the expensive one. You paid for the ad, the click, maybe a welcome discount. The second order is where that spend starts to pay back. Yet many brands put far more effort into acquisition than into the weeks right after someone buys for the first time.

Here's a simple playbook for getting that second order.

### Step 1: Know your natural reorder window

Pull a list of customers who have placed at least two orders and look at the time between their first and second purchase. You'll usually see a cluster. That cluster is your reorder window, and it looks very different for a skincare brand than for an apparel brand.

Your outreach should land just before that window closes, not on a fixed schedule borrowed from someone else.

### Step 2: Make the first experience earn the second

No offer fixes a disappointing first order. Before you design incentives, check the basics:

- Did the order arrive when you said it would?
- Did the customer know how to get the most out of the product?
- Was there an easy way to ask a question or swap a size?

A short post purchase email that helps someone get value from what they bought often does more for repeat orders than a discount.

### Step 3: Choose who gets an incentive

Not every first time buyer needs a reason to come back. Split them:

- **Already reordered or clearly engaged.** Leave them alone, or thank them without a discount.
- **Inside the window, not yet reordered.** A light nudge: new arrivals, a refill reminder, a useful guide.
- **Past the window and quiet.** This is where a funded reward makes sense.

The goal is to spend incentive budget only where it changes behavior.

### Step 4: Design an offer that fits

A good second order offer is:

- **Specific.** Tied to a category the customer already bought or a natural next item.
- **Personal or single use.** So it doesn't leak.
- **Time bound.** A clear end date gives a reason to act now without setting permanent price expectations.
- **Sized to your margin.** Work out what a repeat customer is worth to you over the next year and fund the offer from that, not from a round number.

Free shipping, a gift with purchase, or a credit toward a second item can work as well as a percentage off, and they protect your perceived price.

### Step 5: Put the reward where they'll find it

Email and SMS are the obvious channels, and they're crowded. Think about where else a reward can live: in the box with the first order, on the account page, or in a wallet pass the customer can find later on their phone. An offer that's easy to find when the customer is ready to buy beats one buried in an inbox.

### Step 6: Find new customers who look like your best ones

Second orders also come from people who are new to you but already loyal to brands like yours. If someone shops regularly in your category, a well placed first offer can lead straight into a repeat relationship. The challenge is finding those shoppers without buying lists or collecting data you don't need.

### Plan a pilot

Choose one audience, one reward and one result to measure. Set the budget and eligibility rules before launch. Tell participants which information you need and share results in aggregate where possible.
