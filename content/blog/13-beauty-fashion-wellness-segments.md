# Beauty, fashion and wellness: three stacked segments worth testing

A lot of the brands that reach out to us are in one of three categories: beauty, fashion or wellness. That makes sense. They're some of the most competitive categories in LA, and they're the ones where paying for attention has gotten noticeably more expensive.

So rather than talk about segmentation in the abstract, here are three specific segments I'd test, one for each category. Each one stacks a loyalty membership or tier with merchant, category or card spend in the last 90 days. None of them rely on individual items.

## Why these three categories need a different approach

Start with what it costs to find customers on paid social. Triple Whale's Meta benchmarks, covering more than 40,000 brands from August 2025 to July 2026, break it down by industry ([Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)):

- **Beauty:** median cost per acquisition $39.31, up 6.06%. Median conversion rate 1.79%, down 10.45%.
- **Apparel & Accessories:** median cost per acquisition $36.98, up 1.49%.
- **Health & Wellness:** the highest median CPM in the dataset at $21.80, up 18.96%. Median conversion rate down 13.99%.

The common thread: getting in front of people costs more, and in beauty and wellness, fewer of them are converting. That's a good argument for being more precise about who you're paying to reach.

These are also categories where big loyalty programs already sort shoppers by how much they spend. Sephora, Ulta, Nordstrom and lululemon all have tiered programs. That gives you a clean first signal to stack on.

## Segment one: beauty

**The stack:** Sephora Beauty Insiders in the top spend tier (Rouge) whose cards also show luxury retail spend in the last 90 days.

**Why it works:** Rouge status takes at least $1,000 on Sephora merchandise in a calendar year ([Sephora](https://www.sephora.com/beauty/terms-conditions-beauty-insider)). That tells you this shopper invests in beauty. The luxury retail spend in the last 90 days tells you two more things: they're actively spending now, and they're comfortable at a higher price point.

**Who should test it:** prestige skincare, fragrance and color brands, especially ones priced above drugstore.

**A variant:** if your price point is more accessible, try Ulta Platinum or Diamond members, which take $500 and $1,200 in a calendar year ([Ulta](https://www.ulta.com/rewards/faq)), whose cards show beauty category spend in the last 90 days.

**One thing to watch:** Sephora and Ulta tiers both last through the end of the following calendar year. A tier alone can be stale. That's why the 90-day spend signal matters so much here.

## Segment two: fashion

**The stack:** Nordy Club members at Ambassador status or higher ([Nordstrom](https://www.nordstromrack.com/customer-service/nordyclub)) whose cards show apparel category spend and luxury retail merchant spend in the last 90 days.

**Why it works:** status at a department store program says this shopper buys fashion regularly. Apparel category spend in the last 90 days says they're in buying mode now. Luxury retail spend narrows it to people shopping at a higher price point.

**Who should test it:** LA contemporary and designer labels selling online, and boutiques with an ecommerce arm.

**A variant:** for a lower price point, drop the luxury retail signal and use Nordy Club Influencer members with apparel category spend in the last 90 days.

## Segment three: wellness

**The stack:** lululemon members at Collective Plus or Pinnacle whose cards show fitness studio spend in the last 90 days.

**Why it works:** lululemon's Collective Plus unlocks at $500 in yearly spend and Pinnacle at $1,000 ([lululemon](https://shop.lululemon.com/membership)). That's a shopper who spends real money on how they move. Fitness studio spend in the last 90 days confirms the habit is current, not something from last spring.

**Who should test it:** supplements, recovery tools, activewear, skincare with a wellness angle and anything sold to people who work out a lot.

**A variant:** add natural grocery category spend in the last 90 days as a third signal if your product sits closer to food or nutrition.

## How to run the test

Whichever segment fits your brand, keep the test simple.

1. **One segment.** Don't run all three at once unless you sell across categories.
2. **One reward.** Make it worth having and immediate. Antavo found 49.1% of consumers are disappointed when rewards take too long to earn ([Antavo](https://antavo.com/blog/global-customer-loyalty-report-2026/)).
3. **A clear target.** Set the cost per new customer you'd be happy with, using your own paid social CPA as the comparison.
4. **A follow-up plan.** Know how first-time buyers get into your email list and loyalty program after the first order.
