# Access beats discounts: what 2027 loyalty changes mean for LA brands

Last week United Airlines told MileagePlus members it's changing how people earn elite status in 2027. It's an airline story, but I think every retail and ecommerce brand in LA should read it, because it's the clearest example yet of where big loyalty programs are heading.

The short version: the largest programs in the country are tying status to spend, trimming the free value that used to come easy, and leaning harder on recognition and access. Shoppers are telling researchers that's what they value most. If you run a brand that competes for those same shoppers, that changes what a good reward looks like.

## What actually changed

**United.** Starting Jan. 1, 2027, United will stop counting flight segments toward Premier status. Members will qualify only on Premier qualifying points, which come from eligible flight spending, award travel and United credit card spend. The top tier, Premier 1K, now takes 32,000 qualifying points, up from 28,000 in 2026 ([CNBC, Oct. 13, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/13/united-flight-segments-earn-elite-status.html)). Status is now about how much you spend, full stop.

**Starbucks.** In March 2026, Starbucks moved its program to three levels, Green, Gold and Reserve. Gold takes 500 Stars in a year and Reserve takes 2,500. Your status level now sets how fast you earn Stars, not your payment method. Reward redemptions also picked up dollar caps, such as $6 at the 100 Star tier and $10 at the 200 Star tier ([Starbucks](https://about.starbucks.com/starbucks-rewards-faq/)). Higher levels get more time to redeem birthday rewards, Stars that don't expire and, at Reserve, access to exclusive experiences.

**Dunkin'.** Dunkin's program terms, updated October 2025, say points now expire 12 months from the last day of the month they were earned, regardless of activity. Members with 12 qualifying visits in a calendar month get Boosted Status, earning 12 points per dollar instead of 10 ([Dunkin'](https://www.dunkindonuts.com/dunkin-rewards-terms-and-conditions/)).

Put those side by side and the pattern is hard to miss. Programs are rewarding their heaviest spenders more, capping or expiring the easy value, and making status itself the thing worth having.

## Shoppers are already there

This isn't just the big brands squeezing margin. The research says shoppers want recognition more than they want another coupon.

The [2026 Bond Loyalty Report](https://bondbl.com/news/the-bond-loyalty-report-returns-with-loyalty-decoded-revealing-why-loyalty-has-never-looked-stronger/), which surveyed 20,591 US loyalty program members, found that access, meaning first looks, last calls and front of the line, held its spot as the top loyalty driver in both 2025 and 2026, even with ongoing economic pressure. Bond's read is that customers place more value on how brands recognize and prioritize them, not just how they reward them.

The same report found 85% of consumers are more likely to keep doing business with a brand that has a loyalty program, and 73% spend more as a result. Bond also warns about wallet consolidation: consumer dollars concentrating among fewer preferred brands, with more programs competing for less.

Antavo's 2026 data points the same way. Savings still matter to 70.8% of consumers, but free products or services (46.3%) and personalized rewards (41.6%) were seen as signs of a more mature program, one that offers value beyond discounts ([Antavo](https://antavo.com/news/antavo-gclr-2026-report/)).

## What this means if you're an LA brand

Most LA retail and ecommerce brands I talk to aren't running a United-scale program. Many don't have a loyalty program at all, and the ones that do are usually points plus a discount code. Here's how I'd think about 2027.

**Your best prospects are being trained to value status.** The shopper who's Gold or Reserve at Starbucks, Rouge at Sephora or Diamond at Ulta is used to being recognized for spend. A generic 15% off code reads as noise to them. Early access to a drop or a first look at a collection reads as respect.

**January matters more than usual.** Sephora's VIB and Rouge tiers qualify on calendar-year spend and last through the following calendar year ([Sephora](https://www.sephora.com/beauty/terms-conditions-beauty-insider)). Ulta's Platinum and Diamond work on the same Jan. 1 to Dec. 31 window ([Ulta](https://www.ulta.com/rewards/faq)). lululemon says plainly that a member who doesn't hit their level's minimum will see status adjust on Jan. 1, 2027 ([lululemon](https://shop.lululemon.com/membership)). A lot of high-value shoppers will be rethinking where they spend right at the start of the year.

**Discounts are getting harder to defend.** When the biggest programs cap and expire value, they're telling you something about the economics. If blanket discounts were working, they wouldn't be pulling back.

**Access is cheaper than it looks.** Early access, a reserved restock, a members-only preview: these cost you planning, not margin. For a small brand, they can be the most generous thing you offer.
