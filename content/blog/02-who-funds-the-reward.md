# Who funds the reward and why it pays: the retail brand's side of the trade

When I explain Perkalate to a founder, the first question is almost always the same: who's paying for the reward? It's a fair question. Shoppers get something of value, and somebody has to cover it.

The answer is the brand. On Perkalate, consumer retail and ecommerce brands fund the rewards, and those rewards reach shoppers we've verified through their loyalty membership or tier and their merchant, category or card spend in the last 90 days. Brands pay only for the rewards they fund, and it's free to start. There are 108 brands on the platform today.

That can sound like a cost line. I think it's the opposite, and the loyalty data backs it up. Here's the brand's side of the trade, laid out plainly.

## Rewards already pay when you aim them at the right people

Start with what we know about loyalty spending in general. Antavo's [Global Customer Loyalty Report 2026](https://antavo.com/news/antavo-gclr-2026-report/), a survey of 3,000 marketers and 10,000 consumers, found that 92.7% of loyalty program owners reported a positive return, with an average ROI of 5.3x. 83.0% said they were satisfied with their program's performance.

Brands are voting with budget, too. According to [Antavo's report summary](https://antavo.com/blog/global-customer-loyalty-report-2026/), program owners now put 51.5% of their marketing budget toward CRM and loyalty. And 59.8% said they'd shift even more budget out of short-term promotions and into loyalty if they could ([Antavo](https://antavo.com/news/antavo-gclr-2026-report/)).

The case studies show why. Smile.io publishes customer stories with member and non-member comparisons:

- At Henry's House of Coffee, members deliver 175% higher customer lifetime value than non-members and buy 163% more often per year ([Smile.io](https://smile.io/customers/henry-s-house-of-coffee)).
- At Applied Nutrition, members are worth 215% more in lifetime value and buy 76% more often ([Smile.io](https://smile.io/customers/applied-nutrition)).
- At Live Bearded, members spend 25% more than the average customer, and customers who redeem rewards buy 2.5x more often than those who never have ([Smile.io](https://smile.io/customers/live-bearded)).

None of that means a reward magically creates a better customer. Some of it is selection: people who join and redeem were already your fans. That's the point, though. Rewards work best when they land on people who are already inclined to buy. The hard part has always been finding those people before they've found you.

## Your own program only rewards people you already have

Here's the gap. Every number above describes a brand rewarding its own customers. Henry's program covers 58% of its active customer base ([Smile.io](https://smile.io/customers/henry-s-house-of-coffee)). That's strong coverage, and it's still a ceiling. A loyalty program can't reward someone who hasn't bought yet.

So the money most brands put into loyalty goes to people already on the list. That's good spending. It's also the reason growth gets expensive: the new customer you want is sitting in somebody else's loyalty program, spending in your category, and you can't reach them with the tool that performs best for you.

When a brand funds a reward on Perkalate, it's taking the loyalty mechanic that already works and pointing it at people who look like its best customers but aren't customers yet. A shopper who's a Sephora Beauty Insider at VIB or Rouge, whose card also shows spend in the beauty category in the last 90 days, is a reasonable bet for an LA skincare brand. You're not guessing from an interest graph. You're starting from verified membership and recent spend.

## Why shoppers care about a reward someone else funded

Shoppers don't care who funds a reward. They care whether it's worth having and whether they can actually use it.

Antavo's consumer data is blunt about what goes wrong in programs. The top disappointment, cited by 49.1% of consumers, is that it takes too long to earn rewards. Next is rewards expiring before they can be used (41.1%), then unattractive rewards (38.9%) ([Antavo](https://antavo.com/blog/global-customer-loyalty-report-2026/)). In 2025, 27% of points customers earned went unspent.

A brand-funded reward sidesteps the slowest part of that experience. The shopper doesn't have to grind through a points ladder at a store they haven't visited yet. The brand has decided up front that this person is worth a reward and is paying for it.

It also lines up with what consumers say they want. Savings still motivate 70.8% of consumers, and 46.3% point to free products or services as a sign of a more mature program ([Antavo](https://antavo.com/news/antavo-gclr-2026-report/)). A funded reward from a brand you'd plausibly like is that kind of value.

## What the brand actually gets for the money

Let me be specific about what's in the trade for the brand, because "awareness" isn't an answer.

**A defined audience.** You choose the signals. Membership or tier in a program, plus merchant, category or card spend in the last 90 days. Two or three of those stacked together narrow the audience to people who behave like your best customers.

**Spend you control.** You pay only for the rewards you fund. There's no minimum to get started, so you can fund a small test, look at what happens and decide whether to do more.

**A first purchase with a reason behind it.** The shopper has a concrete reason to try you now, not someday. For a brand that's new to LA shoppers, or launching a line into a crowded category, that reason matters.

**A comparison you can make honestly.** Because you know what you funded and who it went to, you can set it next to what you'd spend chasing the same customer another way and judge it on the numbers.

## The honest caveats

Funding rewards isn't free money, and I don't want to pretend otherwise. A reward that lands on the wrong person is wasted. A reward that's too small won't move anyone, and one that's too rich trains people to wait for the next one. Antavo also found that 31.6% of program owners have trouble working out which purchases happened because of the program ([Antavo](https://antavo.com/blog/global-customer-loyalty-report-2026/)). Attribution is hard everywhere, including here.

What I'd ask any brand to do is the same thing I'd do: start small, stack your signals tightly, decide in advance what a good result looks like and hold the test to it.
