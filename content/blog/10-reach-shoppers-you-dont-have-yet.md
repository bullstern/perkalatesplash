# How an LA ecommerce brand can reach shoppers it doesn't have yet

If you run an ecommerce brand in LA, you probably have a good handle on your existing customers. You email them, text them, maybe run a points program. Those channels are cheap and they work.

The hard part is everyone else. The shopper in Pasadena who'd love your product, buys in your category every month and has never heard of you. Your email list can't reach her. Your loyalty program can't reward her. And the paid channels that can find her keep getting more expensive.

This post is a practical walk-through of how I'd go after that shopper, step by step.

## Where your marketing budget is pointed today

Antavo's 2026 survey of 3,000 marketers found that loyalty program owners put 51.5% of their marketing budget into CRM and loyalty ([Antavo](https://antavo.com/blog/global-customer-loyalty-report-2026/)). Antavo describes the driver as a growing focus on long-term retention over short-term customer acquisition ([Antavo](https://antavo.com/news/antavo-gclr-2026-report/)).

I think that's a smart allocation. Retention pays. But it means more than half the budget at many brands is spent talking to people who already bought.

The rest mostly goes to paid media, where costs are climbing. Triple Whale's benchmarks across more than 40,000 brands show median Meta CPM up 13.24% year over year to $15.06, with median cost per acquisition at $38.99 ([Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)).

So most brands have two modes: talk to people you already have, or pay rising prices to find people you don't. There's room for a third.

## Step one: describe the shopper you want in signals

Start with your own data. Pull your top customers and ask two questions about them: what other programs are they likely members of, and where else do they spend?

Then translate that into signals that can be verified. On Perkalate, that means loyalty membership or tier plus merchant, category or card spend in the last 90 days. Keep it to two or three.

A few examples for LA ecommerce brands:

- **A skincare brand.** Sephora Beauty Insiders at VIB or Rouge whose cards show beauty category spend in the last 90 days.
- **An activewear label.** lululemon members at Collective Plus or Pinnacle whose cards show fitness studio spend in the last 90 days.
- **A fragrance or accessories brand.** Ulta Diamond members whose cards show department store and luxury retail spend in the last 90 days.

None of these depend on knowing what specific item someone bought. They describe a pattern: this person values the category and is spending in it now.

## Step two: give them a reason to try you

A new shopper owes you nothing. You need a reason for her to try you now instead of someday.

That's where a funded reward comes in. On Perkalate, the brand funds the reward and it reaches verified shoppers who match the segment. You pay only for the rewards you fund, and it's free to start.

What should the reward be? Antavo's consumer data gives some direction. 70.8% of consumers say savings motivate them, and 46.3% point to free products or services as a sign of a mature program ([Antavo](https://antavo.com/news/antavo-gclr-2026-report/)). On the downside, 49.1% are disappointed when rewards take too long to earn ([Antavo](https://antavo.com/blog/global-customer-loyalty-report-2026/)). So make the reward immediate and worth noticing.

## Step three: turn the first order into a relationship

The funded reward earns a first purchase. Your own channels have to do the rest.

This is where your existing loyalty and CRM spend earns its keep. Smile.io's customer stories show how much more members are worth once they're in: at Applied Nutrition, members have 215% higher lifetime value than non-members ([Smile.io](https://smile.io/customers/applied-nutrition)); at Henry's House of Coffee, members buy 163% more often per year ([Smile.io](https://smile.io/customers/henry-s-house-of-coffee)).

So plan the handoff. What happens after the first order? Do new buyers get invited into your program? Is there a second-order incentive? Do they hear from you within a week?

## Step four: measure it like a channel

Treat this like any other acquisition test.

- **Cost per new customer.** What you funded divided by the first-time buyers it produced. Compare it with your own paid social cost per acquisition.
- **Repeat rate.** How many of those buyers order again within 60 and 90 days.
- **Segment comparison.** If you run two segments, which one produced better customers, not just more of them?

Be honest about attribution. Antavo found 31.6% of loyalty program owners have trouble working out which purchases happened because of the program ([Antavo](https://antavo.com/blog/global-customer-loyalty-report-2026/)). Keep your test clean: one segment, one reward, one time window.

## Why this matters for LA brands in particular

LA is crowded. Every category has a dozen local brands and a few national ones fighting for the same shoppers. The [2026 Bond Loyalty Report](https://bondbl.com/news/the-bond-loyalty-report-returns-with-loyalty-decoded-revealing-why-loyalty-has-never-looked-stronger/) describes wallet consolidation, with consumer dollars concentrating among fewer preferred brands. If you're not on a shopper's short list soon, you're competing for less.

Reaching verified shoppers with a reason to try you is one of the more direct ways onto that list.

## Getting started

Pick one segment, fund one reward and measure it against what you already spend on paid social. If it works, do more of it.
